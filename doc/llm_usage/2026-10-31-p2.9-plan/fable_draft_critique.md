I read the complete draft plan, Codex's independent design and self-critique, the consultation README and environment inventory, and inspected the adapters the questions touch: the engine's fit path, the recipe validator, the encoders and fit context, the TabM worker, the procedures engine with its cache and channel averaging, the selector, the nested evaluator and its decision function, the pool script's structural counts, the blend logger, and the P2.7 measurement stage. This remains consultation. Nothing was run or written.

The draft is a serious improvement on anything the project had for non-tree methods. Four things in it would, as written, defeat its own goals: the stopping leak test does not test the leak, the learned combiner's "E as one channel" cannot be executed by the engine, the fallback would never fall back to the supported candidate, and the coverage counts are not protected against the deadline. Findings are numbered. "Blocking" means the plan should not go to R2 or into P2.9b with the text as it stands.

## A. Blocking method findings

**1. The stopping-validation label-flip requirement omits the one assertion that tests the leak. Blocking.**
Evidence: section 6.4 says a stopping-validation flip "may change the selected epoch and final fit; only the stopping-training features and fixed-epoch training path must remain unchanged." The leak this adapter exists to prevent is V labels reaching V's own features through `date_te3` fitted on D. S's features never see V labels under any implementation. The draft's assertion passes on the leaky implementation too. Second omission: `inner_split` in `songpop/phase2/tabm.py` and `inner_group_splits` in `encoders.py` are stratified by label, so flipping V labels changes the S/V assignment itself and the test becomes undefined.
Replacement text for 6.4: "With the S/V assignment and S's encoder folds injected and held fixed, flipping any subset of V's labels leaves unchanged: S's feature matrix, V's feature matrix as transformed by S's state, the parameter trajectory of the stopping-stage network at every epoch, and the inner-split receipt. The stopping loss sequence, the chosen epoch and the final fit may change. Positive control: flipping one S label changes the `date_te3` column of S rows sharing that date and leaves V's `date_count` unchanged." Walk-through for the three queries, which the plan should include as a table: a stopping-training row s has features cross-fitted within S with its own group held out and contributes its label to the loss; a stopping-validation row v has features from encoders fitted on all of S and contributes its label only to the stopping loss; an outer-validation row o has features from encoders fitted on D and its label is never read. Only the v assertion above distinguishes a correct adapter from the current engine's behaviour.

**2. "E as one channel" in a learned combiner is not executable. Blocking for section 9.**
Evidence: `channel_cv` in `procedures.py` returns the equal mean of a channel's atoms, and `check_channel_bank` requires member weights equal to one over the atom count. E's atom weights are 1/12, 1/12, 1/12, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4. A channel holding E's six atoms would compute a different predictor than E. The draft's section 9 text "on E as one channel and the admitted atoms" therefore describes a procedure the engine cannot build without a weighted-channel extension.
Correction, two admissible forms, pick one before R2: (a) learned combiners over E's four existing channels plus each admitted atom as its own channel, which is exactly the P2.6b structure and needs no engine change; or (b) a weighted-channel grammar with its own validator, cache request field and rejection tests, which is an engine change that must reproduce the current menu's learned procedures before use. I recommend (a). Note for the cost graph: form (a) puts E's six atoms and every admitted atom in C_L, so the third level rebuilds all of them inside every meta fold.

**3. The new raw-row adapter needs an identity gate on arm B, not only historical replay. Blocking for P2.9b acceptance.**
Evidence: section 6.1 preserves the old adapter for historical recipes and 3 requires "historical baseline reproduction." That verifies the old path, not the new one. On arm B the new adapter performs the same computation as the old one: same context-seeded split, same preprocessing on S then on D, same seeds. If the refactor is correct, their predictions are identical.
Replacement rule: "Acceptance of the neural adapter requires, for each P2.5 timing recipe, that the new adapter on arm B reproduces the old adapter's validation and test predictions within 1e-12 on all 15 folds, in addition to the old adapter reproducing the P2.5 banks. A difference is a defect, not a new result." The same gate applies to any later change of the adapter.

**4. Matched pairs are under-specified and manifests are not deadline-safe. Blocking for the coverage claim, which is Sam's explicit concern.**
Evidence: section 7 requires "at least eight plain and eight embedded settings" for MLP and ResNet and "four B/D pairs." Eight plain and eight embedded settings drawn by a space-filling sampler are not pairs; a difference between them confounds the representation with hyperparameters. Section 11 admits that coverage may be cut for time but the manifests have no ordering rule, so a cut at an arbitrary point leaves an unbalanced design and an uninterpretable "incomplete" status.
Replacement rules for section 7: "A pair is two recipes identical in every field except the one under test. Every representation comparison in this plan is a pair: B against D, plain against embedded, piecewise-linear against periodic at the same backbone, log-loss against AUC stopping. Each family's initial manifest is ordered in blocks, frozen before any score: block 1, controls and mandatory pairs; block 2, the representation branches at the block-1 hyperparameter points; block 3, the stratified space-filling remainder. Execution follows manifest order within a family and the cost gate may stop a family only at a block boundary. A family's coverage status names the last completed block." Under this rule TabM's block 1 is the two old-adapter controls, their arm-B new-adapter twins from finding 3, and four B/D pairs at four backbones; block 2 is the PLE and periodic twins of those four backbones; block 3 fills to 32.

**5. The fallback anchor and the full-refit path are inconsistent with the evaluator as implemented. Must be resolved before P2.9h; the anchor is an owner choice.**
Evidence: section 10 keeps "fallback to H if the selected procedure loses at least 0.003 on average." `nested.decide` compares the selection with `anchor_id`. With E in the menu and already evaluated in P2.7, a selection that lost to E but beat H would never trigger anything, and a selection that lost to H by 0.003 would fall back to a 0.83 anchor while a 0.84 candidate sits in the menu. Separately, section 10 allows omitting the full refit for cost, but `run` always computes `full_refit` and `check_result` requires it on the full path; no such path exists.
Correction: pass E as `anchor_id`, so the rule reads "fallback to E if the selected procedure loses at least 0.003 mean outer AUC to E and loses on all three folds; H is reported as a comparator." This changes the frozen decision rule and needs Sam's word and R2. On the refit: keep it. In this evaluator a full refit of an atomic candidate is one fit per atom per outer fold against fifteen inner fits, so omitting it saves little and would require a new engine path; the draft should strike the omission clause.

**6. Several isolation statements are correct but one is unimplementable as phrased, and one route is missing. Non-blocking once reworded.**
Evidence: 6.3 asks for "a label-permutation or own-group test" for self-retrieval. A permutation test on a trained TabR cannot distinguish self-retrieval from ordinary fitting. The construction test in 6.4 is the right one and should replace the permutation wording. The three-query walk-through for TabR under the group-held-out block construction is sound: a training query in block b retrieves from S minus b with encoders fitted on S minus b, so neither its label nor its group's labels reach any memory feature or memory label; a stopping query retrieves from all of S with S's encoders; an outer query retrieves from D with D's encoders. Two additions are needed: the block assignment is label-stratified, so the self-label test must inject a fixed assignment, as in finding 1; and the positive control should flip a memory row's label in another block and assert that its label embedding and the `date_te3` of rows sharing its date change. The plan should also state the accepted train-time versus inference-time memory size mismatch, since memory at training is S minus one block and at inference is all of D.

## B. Coverage, allocation, cost and metadata findings

**7. Half of the RBF grid sits in degenerate corners. Coverage flaw; fix before manifest freeze.**
Evidence: gamma multipliers {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} of 1/d after standardization. For two random standardized rows the expected squared distance is about 2d. At multiplier 10 the kernel value between typical rows is exp(-20), so every row is its own island and the SVM degenerates toward a one-neighbour rule; at 0.01 the kernel is exp(-0.02) and the machine is effectively linear, duplicating the linear SVM. C from 0.01 to 1000 likewise spends two of six values on near-constant and near-hard-margin machines, and large C fits can run long.
Replacement: gamma multipliers {0.1, 0.3, 1, 3} and C {0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 100}, still 24 per arm, with an explicit `max_iter` cap and a recorded nonconvergence status. The arithmetic above is a property of the RBF kernel on standardized inputs, not a runtime guess.

**8. Calibrating all 48 SVMs with five-fold Platt is the right discovery principle at an avoidable price. Non-blocking; a cheaper complete procedure exists.**
Evidence: 6.2 requires five calibration fits plus one final fit per fold for every candidate, so the initial SVM batch is six fits per fold before any contribution is known. Codex is right that calibrating only standalone winners would miss a complementary weak model.
Proposal preserving breadth and group safety: "Initial calibrated procedure for every kernel candidate: a single group-held-out calibration part, the first fold of five grouped folds of D seeded from the context, exactly as the neural stopping split; the complete recipe fitted on the other four fifths; margins on the held-out fifth standardized and mapped by the section 6.2 sigmoid; the complete recipe refitted on D. Two fits per fold. Nominees are re-evaluated with the five-fold version as a separate recipe before menu entry." The sigmoid has two parameters, so about 620 held-out margins suffice. Add the rejection test that calibrated fold AUC equals margin fold AUC within 1e-12, since the map is monotone; a violation means an orientation or clipping defect.

**9. Redundant branches that buy little information. Non-blocking.**
Linear SVM at six C values duplicates the 48 logistic runs of P2.2 on the same representation. Random Fourier features duplicate Nyström at a sample size where exact kernels are feasible. Gaussian naive Bayes at three smoothing values is one configuration. Smooth logistic with splines, cyclic and date encoding was done in P2.4 to about 0.80, so most of the 24 repeats Finding 022. The "explicitly listed low-order interactions" are not listed anywhere; either name them as the existing six-product `interactions` block or drop the clause. Suggested reallocation: linear SVM 2, RFF 0, naive Bayes 2, smooth logistic 12 with the block named; the freed attention goes to the TabR and FT-Transformer pairs, which are where Sam's concern lies.

**10. Missing optimization and representation axes. Non-blocking, but declare before the sampler runs.**
Batch size is absent for every neural family, although P2.5's best configuration used 128 against the anchor's 256. TabM's installed `make` defaults change with embeddings: `start_scaling_init` becomes "normal" and `n_blocks` 2 when `num_embeddings` is present, while the current factory hard-codes "random-signs"; the plan must say which initialization the embedded recipes use and record it. With embeddings, one-hot categories and the five missing flags must enter through `cat_cardinalities`, not through the numerical embedding, and the unseen-category index must be declared. FT-Transformer should carry the categorical-token against one-hot-numeric-token pair as a declared pair, not a loose ablation. For learning rates at the bottom of the range, the 300-epoch cap may bind; declare the cap per learning-rate band in the manifest rather than diagnosing after the fact.

**11. Allocation rules: one ambiguity, two hidden counts, one weakened convention.**
Route (b) "within 0.002 of the current best standalone across completed families" is undefined: if trees count, no non-tree family can ever use it; if only P2.9 families count, the leading one always does. Replacement: "(b) within 0.002 of the best standalone among P2.9 families completed at that checkpoint, at most two families." Rescue (d) should be stated as "the rescue batch is that family's first refinement batch, not an addition to it." Hidden counts: seed confirmation of two nominees in twelve families is up to 48 additional full 15-fold runs, and the log-loss against AUC stopping pairs are two runs per neural family; state whether both sit inside the 274 or beside it, and put both in the cost graph. The "positive on at least two repeats" condition is weaker than the all-three-repeats convention of plan section 5.6 and Findings 011 to 023; acceptable for exploration, but the plan should say it is a deliberate relaxation. For neural families nominate three recipes rather than two, because a seed-0 ranking among near-ties is dominated by initialization noise and the three-seed confirmation is what resolves it.

**12. Cost structure: the old multipliers are structural; the per-fit cost is what the pilots must measure.**
Codex's critique item 8 says applying the old formula "would under-reserve." The multipliers 3.04 and 14.16 come from fit counts: three outer folds times fifteen inner fits plus one refit for an atom, and five meta folds times fifteen inner fits times three outer folds for a learned channel. Those counts do not change for a calibrated or stopping adapter, because calibration folds and stopping stages happen inside `fit_recipe` and are part of the measured 15-fit run cost. What changes is the per-fit cost at two-thirds population: kernel and GP fits scale faster than linearly in rows, so they get cheaper and the formula over-reserves; networks scale about linearly. So the draft's smaller-population pilot is the right requirement, but the plan should not assert a direction. Two additions: the graph must use the three-seed run time for any neural atom that enters the menu with seeds 0, 1, 2; and sharing is per distinct atom and population, so every `(1-w)E + w atom` candidate shares E's six inner cross-fits and costs only the new atom's. P2.7's 294 fresh cross-fits against 1,476 cache hits is the measured shape of that sharing.

**13. Simplicity metadata and the real bar for selection.**
`supervised()` in `analysis/p26_pool.py` counts only the date target-encoding blocks. HIER is a label-fitted date map and must count; the draft's intent to count a Platt sigmoid and an NCA projection needs that function extended and tested, which section 9 promises. One consequence the plan should state for Sam: E has six atoms and any `(1-w)E + w atom` has seven, so under the frozen rule a new atom changes the final selection only if its blend beats E by more than the 0.001 tie band. The admission threshold of +0.0005 therefore admits candidates the rule will never select. That is the project's intended guard, not a defect, but Sam should know the bar before authorizing the work.

**14. The new outer seed discards a free reproduction check.**
Section 10 saves a new outer assignment at seed 20261102 "as a fresh partition." The new candidates were not chosen on P2.7's outer folds, so reusing seed 20261101 adds no optimism. It gains a strong check: with the final sources reproducing E's atoms, E's nested outer predictions must equal P2.7's within 1e-12, which verifies the refactored engine on the nested route end to end, and it gives paired comparability with Finding 030. Recommendation: keep 20261101 and label it a reused, already-seen partition. R2's call.

**15. An executable structure for "comprehensive" under a deadline.**
The plan can honestly call its design comprehensive and must not call its execution comprehensive until the coverage table says so. Proposed structure: Tranche 0, adapters, rejection tests, the finding-3 gate and score-free pilots at full and two-thirds population, no scores. Tranche 1, block 1 of every family, all 15 folds. Tranche 2, blocks 2 and 3. Tranche 3, seed confirmation of nominees, reserved before tranche 2 starts and taken from whatever is complete at the coverage checkpoint. Tranche 4, refinements. Then menu, R2, nested build and replay. Status vocabulary per family and branch: complete; block n complete, later blocks incomplete; adapter passed, no scores because of measured cost; adapter not finished; not started. The fallback the draft already states is correct and should be repeated in section 12: E with its P2.7 evidence remains the supported production choice, and no new candidate is described as nested-validated unless the revised evaluation and its replay finished.

**16. Dependencies.** The source list cites `rtdl-revisiting-models`, which is not installed. The installed `tabm` module exposes `MLPBackbone`, and a ResNet block, a compact FT-Transformer and a compact TabR are short plain-torch modules inside the existing worker. Writing them avoids a new hash-locked route and its review. If Codex prefers the package, that is a P2.1-style lock review, not Sam's authority, and the plan should say which way it goes.
